# MailFlow AI — Brand Voice and Tone Guide

**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** Content Lead
**Last updated:** April 2026

---

## Who We Are

MailFlow AI is the email marketing tool for people who care about design, hate corporate software, and want AI to do the heavy lifting — not just suggest subject lines.

We're built for creators, indie hackers, freelancers, and small business owners who run their business from their phone and have zero patience for tools that look like enterprise software from 2009.

We're direct. We're real. We have a point of view.

---

## Brand Archetype: The Outlaw + The Magician

**The Outlaw** (primary): We challenge the status quo. We call out what's broken. We exist because the incumbents stopped caring about creators. We're not going to pretend Mailchimp is fine. It's not.

**The Magician** (secondary): We transform a chore into a superpower. Type one sentence. Get a complete email campaign. That's not just a feature — it's a magic trick, delivered instantly. We make hard things feel effortless.

The combination: disruptive and empowering. We're not angry for anger's sake — we're angry because there's a better way and we built it.

---

## The Voice: "That Friend Who's Really Good at Marketing"

Imagine a friend who runs a successful newsletter. They're not a guru. They don't have a course to sell you. They just know what works and they tell you directly because they want you to win.

That's our voice.

They say: "Hey, Mailchimp is going to charge you for people who already left your list — just so you know."

They don't say: "We've identified a suboptimal pricing structure in legacy email marketing solutions that may impact your growth trajectory."

They show you something cool and explain why it matters. They don't oversell it. They let the thing speak for itself.

## Core Voice Attributes

| Attribute | What It Means | Example |
|-----------|---------------|---------|
| **Direct** | No preamble. Lead with the point. | "Your open rate is determined by your subject line. Not your body copy. Write accordingly." |
| **Confident** | We have a point of view. We state it without hedging. | "Mailchimp is designed for enterprise. You're not enterprise. Stop paying like you are." |
| **Specific** | Numbers over adjectives. Show, don't claim. | "500 contacts. 1,000 emails. 5 AI writes. Free." Not "generous free plan." |
| **Irreverent** | Light attitude toward the competition. Never mean, always factual. | "Their AI feature was added in a sprint. Ours was the founding idea." |
| **Empowering** | The user is the hero. We're the tool. | "Write like you. Not like a press release." |

## What We Never Say

These words and phrases are banned from MailFlow AI copy. If you catch yourself writing any of these, stop and rewrite.

| Banned | Why | Use Instead |
|--------|-----|-------------|
| "leverage" | Corporate. Hollow. | "use" |
| "synergy" | Meaningless. | Delete the sentence. |
| "holistic solution" | Enterprise speak. | Name the specific thing. |
| "cutting-edge" | Every company says this. | Name what makes it modern. |
| "seamless" | Says nothing. | Describe the actual experience. |
| "empower" (as noun phrase) | Overused. | Show it instead. |
| "industry-leading" | Unverifiable fluff. | Use actual data or don't say it. |
| "innovative" | If you have to say it... | Name the actual innovation. |
| "intuitive" | Usability claims need proof. | Describe the interaction. |
| "robust" | Meaningless in every context. | Say what the thing actually does. |
| "passionate about" | Every company is "passionate." | Cut or show the evidence. |
| "journey" (marketing usage) | Cringe. | Use a real word for what it is. |
| "delight" | Overused in SaaS. | Let the product delight. Don't announce it. |

## Tone by Channel

Tone adapts to context. Voice stays constant. Here's how each channel should feel:

---

### TikTok / Reels

**Tone:** Raw, unfiltered, slightly confrontational. Like talking to camera at 11pm after a good day.

**Rules:**
- First 3 words must grab. No setup.
- One point per video. Not five.
- Speak in second person: "your email tool," "you're overpaying," "you should know this."
- End with either a question (comments) or a CTA to the link in bio. Never both — pick one.
- Never start with "Hey guys." Never.

**Example (good):**
> "Mailchimp charges you for people who left your list. That's not a bug. It's how they make money. MailFlow doesn't. Free plan in bio."

**Example (bad):**
> "Hey guys! Today I want to talk about something that's been on my mind — email marketing tools and why I think there might be some better options out there for creators like you who are looking to grow your audience..."

---

### Twitter / X

**Tone:** Sharp. One idea. Leaves room for the reader to think.

**Rules:**
- Short paragraphs. Never a wall of text.
- Use line breaks to create rhythm.
- Lead with the most interesting or provocative sentence.
- Numbers add credibility. Use them.
- Question tweets should be genuinely curious, not performative.

**Example (good):**
> Email marketing tools are designed for enterprise.
>
> You're not enterprise.
>
> Stop paying like you are.

**Example (bad):**
> Excited to share that MailFlow AI is now live! We've built an amazing product that we think creators will love. Check it out and let us know what you think! 🚀

---

### LinkedIn

**Tone:** Still direct. Still real. But slightly more considered — this is professional context. The irreverence is there but turned down from 10 to 7.

**Rules:**
- Open with a claim or observation that would make someone stop scrolling.
- Use personal voice — "I built this" not "we have developed"
- It's okay to be a little vulnerable here. Share the real story.
- Numbers and specifics even more important than on other platforms (professional audience expects it)
- No hashtag spam — max 3, relevant ones only.

**Example (good):**
> I watched a creator spend 40 minutes writing a welcome email.
>
> She hated every word she wrote.
>
> She deleted it and sent nothing.
>
> That's the email marketing problem no one talks about. The tools don't just charge too much — they make the act of writing feel hard.
>
> MailFlow AI gives you a full email draft from one sentence. Edit 20%. Send.
>
> We shipped it because that 40-minute problem is everywhere.

**Example (bad):**
> Thrilled to announce the launch of MailFlow AI, an innovative AI-powered email marketing platform designed to empower creators and small business owners with cutting-edge tools to streamline their email marketing journey!

---

### Email (Our Own Campaigns)

**Tone:** One step more personal than everything else. This is 1:1. Write like you know them.

**Rules:**
- Subject line: one idea, usually a curiosity gap or specific number
- Preview text: continues the subject, adds information — never restates it
- Body: short paragraphs, never longer than 3 sentences each
- One CTA per email. One. Not two. Not "or you can also."
- Sign off as a person, not a brand. "— Ralph" not "The MailFlow Team"
- Plain text energy even in HTML templates — avoid over-designed emails

**Subject line examples (good):**
- "Your first email is waiting"
- "The thing Mailchimp doesn't tell you"
- "3 weeks in. Here's what we learned."
- "Open rate: 34%. Here's how."

**Subject line examples (bad):**
- "Exciting news from MailFlow AI!"
- "Your monthly product update"
- "Important information about your account"
- "We've been working hard and can't wait to share..."

---

### Landing Page Copy

**Tone:** Clear and confident. Every word earns its place. Headlines should work without subheadings.

**Rules:**
- Hero headline: specific promise. Not a tagline. A statement of value.
- Subheading: expand the headline with one more piece of information.
- Feature copy: lead with the benefit, follow with the feature.
- CTA: action-first. "Start free" not "Get started for free today."
- Social proof: specific numbers, not vague claims.

**Example (good):**
Headline: "Type one sentence. Get a complete email campaign."
Sub: "MailFlow AI writes your subject line, preview text, and full email body in seconds. Powered by Claude AI."
CTA: "Start free"

**Example (bad):**
Headline: "The Future of Email Marketing is Here"
Sub: "Leverage our cutting-edge AI-powered platform to take your email campaigns to the next level."
CTA: "Get Started Today!"

## Specific Rules for Writing About Competitors

Competitors are real. We can reference them. Rules:

1. Only state facts. "Mailchimp counts unsubscribed contacts toward your plan limit" — verified, factual.
2. Never mock the team or people. Mock the decision, not the company or its founders.
3. If we claim superiority, name what specifically is better and be prepared to show it.
4. Never use terms like "inferior," "outdated," or "bad." Let the facts make the case.
5. Default framing: "They built for X. We built for Y." Comparative, not combative.

## How We Write About AI

MailFlow AI uses Claude (Anthropic) for email generation. Here's how we talk about it:

**Honest framing:**
- "Claude AI writes a draft. You edit it to sound like you."
- "5 AI-generated emails per day on the free plan."
- "AI is a first draft, not a finished product."

**We don't claim:**
- "AI writes perfect emails" — never
- "AI replaces copywriters" — never
- "Our AI knows your audience" — it doesn't; be honest

The AI is a tool that removes the blank-page problem. We say that. We don't over-promise.

## The Shareability Test

Before publishing any piece of content, ask: "Would someone screenshot this and share it?"

Good reasons to share:
- It said something true that most people feel but haven't heard said this clearly
- It showed me something I didn't know
- It made me laugh or nod
- It validated something I've been thinking

Bad reasons to share:
- It's hype about a product I already bought
- It told me something I already knew
- It was inspirational in a vague, forgettable way

Every piece of content we create should clear the shareability test. If it doesn't, rewrite it until it does.

## Quick Reference Card

**Do:**
- Lead with the user's pain, not our features
- Use specific numbers whenever possible
- Write short sentences
- State opinions confidently
- Let silence do work (don't fill every pause with adjectives)
- Sound like a person

**Don't:**
- Use corporate jargon (see banned list)
- Write long setups before the point
- Over-promise on AI capabilities
- Claim things you can't verify
- Use passive voice as a hedge
- Write in third person ("MailFlow AI believes...")
- Sound excited about your own product (show it, don't announce it)
